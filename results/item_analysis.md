## Discrimination

Item-total discrimination is the point-biserial correlation, across the 4 retrievers, between the item's 0/1 result and each retriever's total score on the other 62 items (corrected item-total). It is undefined (n/a) when the item has zero variance across retrievers (all correct or all wrong) or when the rest scores are all equal.

## Limitations

- The 4 retrievers are not independent raters. TF-IDF, BM25, BM25+ and RRF (which fuses TF-IDF and BM25) share one tokenizer and one 68-document index, so high agreement is expected by construction and says little about item quality.
- With 4 respondents a corrected item-total correlation rests on 4 data points and takes only a few values; it is a descriptive flag, not an estimate with a usable confidence interval. No interval is reported.
- Difficulty is on a 5-level scale (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) because there are 4 retrievers, and it is dominated by the 0 level.
- Kappa is undefined when a retriever is constant, and is unstable when agreement is near-total and the base rate is low (the kappa paradox); read the raw agreement counts alongside it.
- Factual items only (63), one run per retriever; category-error items are scored by declining and are excluded.
- No language model has been evaluated, so these item statistics describe difficulty for lexical retrieval only and must not be read as difficulty for an LLM. Item parameters (IRT, Rasch) are not fitted: 4 respondents cannot support them.
