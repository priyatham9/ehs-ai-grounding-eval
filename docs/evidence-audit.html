03 / WHAT THE EXPERIMENT CHANGESTHREE DISTINCTIONS WORTH KEEPING

A retrieval miss is not one problem.

I.

Finding a source ≠
answering a question.

An attached source can be authoritative and still be too short for a multi-part question. Inspect the LOTO inspection item: the excerpt supplies the interval, but omits who may inspect.

II.

The wrapper is part
of the experiment.

The returned text includes a section title and clause reference. Removing this metadata changes which questions can pass. A title can supply relevant context; clause numbers also enter the numeric matcher. Format sensitivity alone is not proof of contamination.

III.

A ceiling is conditional.
So is a pass.

These results hold for this excerpt set, response template, and scorer. Longer passages, synthesized answers, or different scoring rules require a new experiment. No LLM was evaluated here.

04 / METHOD & PROVENANCETAKE THE FINDING APART

Reproducible.
And deliberately bounded.

All results are derived from the committed corpus and deterministic scoring code. The answer-key-aware search is an audit instrument: it asks what the current index could possibly score, given these rules.

Inspect the source repository
Scope
63 factual questions; 68 indexed excerpts. Five category-error questions excluded from the question set, retained in the index.
Experiment
Every factual question scored against every excerpt, with and without the adapter’s metadata wrapper.
Acceptance rule
At least 75% concept coverage, all required numeric facts, and no asserted forbidden concept. Earlier scorer gates also apply.
Not a ranker comparison
The random floor reads the answer key when constructing responses. It is not a comparable retrieval baseline and is excluded here.
